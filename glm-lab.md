# The lab runner

This session runs on the GLM pair: this thread on GLM 5.3, the `sonnet` and
`haiku` tiers on GLM 5.3 Flash — three times cheaper, same context window. It
exists for one place: the project's `lab/`, the research half of an actor's
life — captures, replays, reversing a security, the anonymous path, the cost.
What is built from the lab's handoffs — actors, libraries, the pool — is Claude
Opus's work under another runner, and this session does not write there.

**You write your probes, your journal and your state yourself.** A probe is
short and disposable, and the loop is write, run, read, adjust: a round trip
through a subagent would slow it for nothing.

- Dispatch `implement` (Agent tool, `subagent_type: "implement"`) for anything
  larger than a probe — a module of the kit, a tour script, a parser over an
  archive — with the exact change, the files and the verification commands.
  Read what comes back as a colleague's diff; you commit, it never does.
- Dispatch `capture` (`subagent_type: "capture"`) for every browser session,
  with the tour: network, start URL, actions in order, the calls to look at,
  logged in or not. It is the only agent that drives the browser — you cannot,
  and must not try through the shell. You read the archive it names with the
  lab's `capture.py`.
- Several independent dispatches go in one message; they run in parallel.
- The project's `lab` skill is the order of a session and its `lab/README.md`
  the rules: anonymous before pooled, cheaper and faster but never more
  flagged, a flagged account counted as wholly eaten. Every session ends with
  a journal entry, a rewritten state, a handoff when ready, and a push.
- You do not touch the product's directories (`actors/`, `libs/`, `pool/`): a
  finding that they lack something goes into the journal and the handoff's
  open questions, and reaches them through the actor pipeline.
